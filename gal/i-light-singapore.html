Grab a spray can, press the nozzle, and create anything you can imagine on the five-metre-tall canvas. This is graffiti without the paint: no aerosols and no mess — yet the experience still feels entirely tactile.

And the crowds have shown up. Infinite Graffiti has become one of the festival's standout draws — every night, families line up to pick up a spray can and add their mark to the wall. The visitors include Singapore's Senior Minister Lee Hsien Loong and the Minister for National Development, who stepped up alongside everyone else to paint on the canvas.

It's not only families and dignitaries — Singapore's own graffiti community has embraced the wall too. Local writer Antz came down to paint, and so did the crew dplmt — proof that the experience holds up for seasoned writers as much as for first-timers.

Over three weeks, thousands are getting their chance to create their own unique artwork. Presented by Families for Life as part of the National Family Festival, Infinite Graffiti has transformed into a collective canvas where families create and connect. By removing traditional barriers, we invite people of all generations to step up as artists, turning moments of play into lasting memories.

Eighteen years. Six continents. One idea, done right.

For eighteen years, Graffiti+ has been putting creative digital graffiti tools into people's hands — in public, at scale, and always with cultural integrity.

Born from the roots of graffiti culture and built for anyone willing to pick up a custom spray can, the platform transforms public spaces into shared canvases where individuals and groups paint together in real time. From their home in Vancouver to projects across six continents, Graffiti+ has partnered with world-renowned brands like Louis Vuitton, Nike, Chanel, Hennessy, and Google to bring authentic, interactive experiences to audiences everywhere.

The goal has always been the same: to create a space for real expression and culture that is open to everyone.
